The Unknown

The questions we can't yet answer. The darkness between the light.

Science has given us a map of the universe. It is a beautiful map, precise to eleven decimal places. And it is mostly blank.

We know the universe is 13.8 billion years old. We know it is expanding, and that the expansion is accelerating. We know that the matter we can see — stars, planets, gas, dust, you, me — is less than 5% of what's out there.

The rest is the Unknown. Not because we haven't looked. Because we don't yet know how to look.

Every answer reveals a deeper question. Trace the lines between what we know.

Each point of light is a question. Each line is a guess.

01

The Edge of the Universe

What we know

We can see 13.8 billion light years in every direction. The cosmic microwave background tells us the universe was once hotter and denser than anything we can imagine.

What we don't

What lies beyond the visible edge? Is there an edge at all, or does space simply continue forever? Could there be other universes beyond our own?

  • If the universe is finite, what is on the other side of its boundary?
  • If it is infinite, how can something infinite have a beginning?
  • Does "beyond" even mean anything if there is no space beyond space?
02

Where Is Space?

What we know

Space is not empty. It bends around mass, carries gravitational waves, and expands faster than light can cross it. Quantum fields ripple through it at every point.

What we don't

What is space made of? Is it a thing, a property, or simply the absence of things? Why does it have a speed limit? Why does it expand at all?

  • If space is expanding, what is it expanding into?
  • Is space continuous, or is it made of discrete units we haven't detected?
  • Can space exist without anything inside it?
03

Dark Matter

What we know

Roughly 85% of the matter in the universe is invisible. We know it exists because of its gravitational pull on galaxies. Without it, galaxies would fly apart.

What we don't

We have never seen it, touched it, or detected a single particle of it. We don't know if it's one thing or many things. We don't even know what kind of thing it is.

  • Is dark matter a particle we haven't found, or something stranger?
  • If we can never see it, can we ever really understand it?
  • What if dark matter is a misunderstanding of gravity itself?
04

Are We Alone?

What we know

There are more planets in the observable universe than grains of sand on Earth. Many are in the habitable zone of their stars. The ingredients for life are everywhere.

What we don't

Yet we have heard nothing. No signals, no biosignatures, no evidence. The silence is the loudest answer science has given us. Is life rare, or are we simply not listening correctly?

  • Is life a cosmic inevitability, or a miracle we haven't recognised?
  • If we are not alone, why have we found no evidence?
  • If we are alone, what does that mean for the value of being here at all?

These are not failures of science. They are the edges of it.

Every question we cannot yet answer is a place where the map runs out. And at the edge of every map, there is a horizon — a line we can point to but never reach, because the horizon itself moves as we approach it.

The Unknown is not empty. It is the most honest thing we have.

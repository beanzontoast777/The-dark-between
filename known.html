Light Years

A light year is the distance light travels in one Earth year. It is about 9.46 trillion kilometres. When we look at a star, we are looking into the past. The light from Proxima Centauri, our nearest stellar neighbour, takes 4.24 years to reach us. We see it as it was, not as it is.

9.46T kilometres per light year
4.24 years to Proxima Centauri
13.8B years of observable light

The Milky Way

Our home galaxy contains over 100 billion stars. It is a barred spiral galaxy, and we sit about 26,000 light years from the centre. Yet, the entire Milky Way is just one of an estimated 200 billion galaxies in the observable universe.

100B stars in our galaxy
26,000 light years to the centre
200B galaxies in the observable universe
